Did your joiner call three times about one quote this week?

Contractor Quote Request Prompt for Interior Designers: Get Priced the First Time

By Mrs. Autopilot · 4 min · No coding

You send a drawing and a line like "can you quote this?". Then the questions start: what size exactly, which veneer, painted or oiled, who fits it, when is the site ready? Each round of back-and-forth is easily 30 minutes. Four quotes a week and it's two hours, and the prices still come back hard to compare.

We work on the workshop side, where these requests land. The ones we can price straight away always cover the same few things. One prompt puts them into every request you send.

Here's the fix

1. Collect the basics

Before you write to anyone, have these ready: item and room, sizes (and whether they're final site sizes or from drawings), material and finish, hardware, who installs (the contractor or someone else), site ready date and target install date. Attach the drawing and 2 to 3 photos of the space.

2. Paste this prompt

Open Claude or ChatGPT (a free account works), start a new chat and paste this prompt with your notes at the end:

I'm an interior designer requesting a price from a contractor (joiner, metal fabricator, stone or glass). Turn my notes below into a short, clear quote request email. Structure: item and room; sizes and whether they are final site measurements or from drawings; material and finish; hardware; who installs; site ready date and target install date; attachments. Ask for the price, lead time and what is not included. If anything from this list is missing in my notes, list it at the end under "Still to confirm" instead of guessing. Under 150 words, friendly and professional. Add a one-line subject starting with "Quote request:". My notes: [paste your notes]

3. Fill the gaps

Read the "Still to confirm" list and answer it yourself before you send. Check every size and finish against your drawing. AI can misread notes, so never send without this check.

4. Send the same request to everyone

If you're getting two or three prices, send every contractor the same email. Then you're comparing like for like, not three different guesses.

What the workshop side wishes you knew

A complete request gets priced first, because it's the easiest one in the inbox. "From drawings, final survey to follow" is fine for a budget price, just say so.

A note on privacy

Don't paste client home addresses, phone numbers, contract values you're not allowed to share or anything confidential into an AI chat. Use first names or initials and add the real details in your own email. Follow your studio's and your clients' rules on AI tools.

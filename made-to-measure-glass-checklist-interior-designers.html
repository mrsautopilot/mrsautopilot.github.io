Have you ever had a glass panel arrive 8 mm too big?

Made-to-Measure Glass: The Measuring Checklist Your Fabricator Wishes You Had

By Mrs. Autopilot · 5 min · No coding

A frameless shower screen measured from the drawings before the tiles went on. A balustrade ordered without knowing the floor finish. A shower door that opens the wrong way because nobody said which side the hinges go. Each one means a remake, and a remade panel can easily cost several hundred dollars plus weeks of delay.

We work on the workshop side, where designers' drawings turn into glass. Most remakes come from the same few missing details. Here's the checklist, and a prompt that puts it into every quote request so you also skip the 30-minute phone call of questions.

Here's the fix

1. Measure the finished site, not the plan

Final glass sizes should be taken after tiling, wall finishes and floor finishes are done. Use the plan only for a rough quote and say so. For the order, ask the fabricator to do (or confirm) a final site survey.

2. The checklist

Sizes: width and height measured at three points (top, middle, bottom / left, middle, right), because walls are rarely straight. Out of plumb: note if a wall leans or the floor slopes. Glass: type (clear, low-iron, tinted, frosted), thickness if specified, any coating. Opening: hinge side and which way the door opens, described as seen from outside the shower or room. Hardware: finish (chrome, brushed nickel, black, brass) and style. Fixing: what's behind the wall where hinges or channels go. Photos: the finished opening, with a tape measure in shot. Dates: when the site is ready for survey and the target install date.

3. Paste this prompt

Open Claude or ChatGPT (a free account works), start a new chat and paste this prompt:

I'm an interior designer requesting a quote for made-to-measure glass from a fabricator. Turn my notes below into a short, clear quote request email. Use this structure: item and location; site status (finished or not, and when it will be ready for a final survey); sizes (and whether they're final site measurements or from drawings); glass type and thickness; hinge side and opening direction as seen from outside; hardware finish; fixing/backing; photos attached; target install date. If anything from this list is missing in my notes, list it at the end under "Still to confirm" instead of guessing. Under 150 words, professional. Add a one-line subject. My notes: [paste your notes]

4. Check before you send

Read the "Still to confirm" list and fill the gaps. Double-check every number against your notes. Send the same email to every fabricator you're getting quotes from, so the quotes are comparable.

Good to know

Glass safety rules (toughened or laminated glass, balustrade heights and loads) depend on your country and local building code. Ask the fabricator to confirm their spec meets it.

A note on privacy

Don't paste client home addresses, phone numbers, contract values you're not allowed to share or anything confidential into an AI chat. Use first names or initials and add the real details in your own email. Follow your studio's and your clients' rules on AI tools.

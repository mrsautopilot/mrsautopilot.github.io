Is a supplier's "we'll let you know" holding up your whole install?

Supplier Follow-Up Email for Interior Designers: Get a Delivery Date in 20 Seconds

By Mrs. Autopilot · 4 min · No coding

The sofa, the dining chairs, the light fittings: each has a delivery date that's somehow still a mystery, and the install is booked. So you write "Hi, just checking in on the order...", rewrite it so it doesn't sound annoyed, and hit send. Fifteen minutes per chase, six chases a week: an hour and a half, every week.

We work on the other side of these emails, in the workshop. The ones that get a fast answer are short, quote the order number, give the install date and ask one clear question with a deadline. One prompt writes exactly that.

Here's the fix

1. Grab three details

Before you write anything, have these ready: the order or PO number and item, your install or handover date, and the one thing you need (a confirmed delivery date, a tracking number, a sample, a revised drawing).

2. Paste this prompt

Open Claude or ChatGPT (a free account works), start a new chat and paste this prompt:

Write a short, polite follow-up email to a supplier. I'm an interior designer. Order: [order/PO number and item]. Our install date is [date]. I need: [confirmed delivery date / tracking / other]. Ask ONE clear question and ask for an answer by [day]. Mention the order number in the subject. Under 70 words, friendly and professional, not passive-aggressive, no "just checking in". If this is the second follow-up, add one sentence saying the install date depends on it.

3. Check and send

Check the order number, item and dates are exactly right. Reply in the same chat with "shorter" or "firmer" if you want a different tone. Send it to the person who confirmed the order, not a general inbox, if you have a name.

4. Put a reminder in your calendar

Add a 5-minute reminder for the deadline day. No answer by then? Run the prompt again and write "second follow-up".

Why this works

Suppliers handle dozens of orders. A subject line with the order number and a single question with a date is the easiest email in their inbox to answer, so it gets answered first.

A note on privacy

Don't paste client home addresses, phone numbers, contract values you're not allowed to share or anything confidential into an AI chat. Use first names or initials and add the real details in your own email. Follow your studio's and your clients' rules on AI tools.

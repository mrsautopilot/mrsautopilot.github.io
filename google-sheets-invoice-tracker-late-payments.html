Scrolling through invoices to see who still hasn't paid?

Google Sheets Invoice Tracker That Flags Late Payments in Red Automatically

By Mrs. Autopilot · 5 min · No coding

Chasing payments is awkward enough. Figuring out who to chase shouldn't be a job of its own: scrolling rows, comparing dates, hoping you didn't miss one.

One rule in Google Sheets turns every late, unpaid invoice red by itself, every single day. No new app, no coding.

Here's the fix

If you track invoices in a spreadsheet, you don't need a new app to see who's late. One conditional formatting rule turns every overdue, unpaid invoice red automatically, every day.

1. Set up your columns

In row 1: A Client, B Invoice #, C Amount, D Due date, E Status. Type real dates in column D. In column E write paid or unpaid (tip: select column E → Insert → Dropdown, and add the options paid and unpaid so the spelling is always the same).

2. Add the rule

Select your rows, for example A2:E200. Click Format → Conditional formatting. In the panel on the right, under "Format cells if…" choose Custom formula is.

3. Paste this formula exactly

=AND($D2<TODAY(),$E2<>"paid")

Pick a red or soft pink fill colour and click Done. Any row whose due date has passed and isn't marked paid now turns red, and it updates on its own every day.

Using Excel?

Select the rows → Home → Conditional Formatting → New Rule → "Use a formula to determine which cells to format" → paste the same formula → Format → Fill → red → OK.

4. Your Monday routine

Open the sheet, look for red, send reminders. Need help with the wording? Paste this into ChatGPT:

Write a short, friendly payment reminder email for an invoice that is [X] days overdue. Keep it under 70 words, polite but clear, no guilt-tripping. Use placeholders [Name], [invoice number], [amount] and [due date]. Add a one-line subject.

Save the result as a Gmail template so next time it's two clicks. When a client pays, change the status to paid and the red disappears.

A note on privacy

Your invoice sheet stays in your own Google account. If you use ChatGPT for the reminder text, don't paste real client names, amounts, bank details or invoice numbers – keep the placeholders and fill them in inside your email. Follow your company's rules on who can access the sheet and on AI tools.

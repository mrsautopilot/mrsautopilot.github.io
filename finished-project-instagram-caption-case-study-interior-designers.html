How many finished projects are sitting in a folder because you haven't written the post yet?

Finished Project Post for Interior Designers: Instagram Caption, Carousel and Case Study From One Prompt

By Mrs. Autopilot · 4 min · No coding

The project is done, the photographer delivered beautiful images, and then... nothing. Writing the Instagram caption, the carousel text and a case study for your website takes about two hours per project, so it waits. Two projects a month is four hours, and your best work stays invisible exactly when new clients are looking.

You already wrote most of the story in your brief and site notes. One prompt turns those notes into all three texts. You check the facts, add your personal touch and post.

Here's the fix

1. Gather what you already have

Open the project brief, your notes on key design decisions (layout changes, materials, colors, custom pieces) and the list of trades and suppliers who worked on it. Pick 6 to 10 of your best photos. Use the client's first name or "the client", never their full name or address.

2. Paste this prompt

Open Claude or ChatGPT (a free account works), start a new chat and paste this prompt with your notes at the end:

I'm an interior designer. Below are my brief and notes for a finished project. Write three things. 1) An Instagram caption under 120 words: start with the problem the client had, then 2 to 3 design decisions and why I made them, end with one soft question to readers. 2) Text for a 5-slide carousel: slide 1 a short hook, slide 2 the before or the problem, slide 3 the key design decisions, slide 4 materials and finishes, slide 5 "save this for your own project". 3) A 150-word case study for my website with the headings Brief, Challenge, Solution, Result. Use only facts from my notes; if something is missing, write [add] instead of guessing. Add a credits line with placeholders like [joiner], [glazier], [stone]. Warm and confident, no clichés like "dream home" or "elevated". Don't name the client or the address. My notes: [paste your brief and notes]

3. Check every fact

Read all three texts against your notes. Fill in each [add], check material names, sizes and finishes, and cut anything that doesn't sound like you. Want a different tone? Reply "shorter", "more personal" or "more technical" in the same chat.

4. Credit your trades

Swap the placeholders for the real joiner, glazier, stone fabricator and suppliers, and tag them on Instagram. We work on the workshop side of projects like these: trades love seeing their work credited and often reshare the post to their own followers, which puts your project in front of new people.

5. Post and reuse

Use the caption and carousel text on Instagram, and the case study on your website portfolio page. Save the prompt in a note so it's ready for the next finished project.

A note on privacy

Always get the client's permission before posting photos of their home. Don't paste home addresses, phone numbers, budgets you're not allowed to share or anything confidential into an AI chat. Use real project photos, not AI-generated images, and follow your studio's and your clients' rules on AI tools.

Do your site visit notes turn into an hour of sorting before any trade sees them?

Snag List Template for Interior Designers: From Site Visit Notes to Every Trade in 10 Minutes

By Mrs. Autopilot · 4 min · No coding

You walk the site with a notebook, your phone and a voice memo running. Paint touch-ups in the hall, a cabinet door that doesn't close, a scratched shower screen, two sockets in the wrong place. Back at the desk you rewrite it all, sort it by trade and send separate emails. That's easily an hour per visit. Two visits a week and it's two hours.

We work on the workshop side, where snag emails arrive. The ones that get fixed fast list only that trade's items, say which room and what needs doing, and ask for a date. One prompt writes exactly that.

Here's the fix

1. Take notes like you already do

On site, jot each snag as you see it: room, what's wrong, and who you think should fix it. Photos with a quick caption are fine. Messy is fine, for example "bath – glass scratched lower left – glazier?".

2. Paste this prompt

Open Claude or ChatGPT (a free account works), start a new chat and paste this prompt:

I'm an interior designer. Below are my rough notes from a site visit. Turn them into a snag list. Group the items by trade (for example joiner, painter, electrician, plumber, glazier, flooring, other). For each item write: room – what's wrong – what needs to be done – priority (high / medium / low). If my notes don't say which trade or room, write [check] instead of guessing. Then write one short, polite email per trade that lists only their items and asks them to confirm a fix date by [date]. Clear and neutral, no blame, under 120 words per email. Add a one-line subject starting with "Snag list:". My notes: [paste your notes]

3. Check the list

Read every line against your notes and photos. Fill in each [check] yourself. AI can misread messy notes, so never send without this check. Want it shorter or firmer? Reply in the same chat.

4. Send one email per trade

Copy each trade's email into your own email, attach the relevant photos and send it to the person who did the work. Each trade sees only their items, so nothing gets lost in a long group email.

5. Track the fixes

Paste the snag list into a simple table or your project folder with a "fixed" column. On the next site visit, walk the list and tick items off. Save the prompt in a note so it's ready after every visit.

A note on privacy

Don't paste client home addresses, phone numbers, contract values you're not allowed to share or anything confidential into an AI chat. Use first names or initials and add the real details in your own email. Follow your studio's and your clients' rules on AI tools.

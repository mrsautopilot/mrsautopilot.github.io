Typing the same email for the sixth time today?

Gmail Templates for Repeated Emails: Set Up in 2 Clicks, No Coding

By Mrs. Autopilot · 10 min · No coding

"Here's the invoice." "Can we reschedule?" "Thanks, received." You write them so often you could do it asleep, and yet every single time you start from a blank screen. Five minutes here, five there, and suddenly it's an hour a week.

Gmail already has a hidden button that turns any of these into a 2-click reply. Most people never switch it on.

Here's the fix

You probably write the same 5–10 emails every week: invoice sent, rescheduling, "thanks, received", meeting follow-up, "just checking in". Gmail can store them as templates so you never retype them.

1. Turn templates on (once)

Open Gmail on a computer → click the gear icon (top right) → See all settings → Advanced tab → next to Templates choose Enable → scroll down and click Save Changes.

2. Save your first template

Click Compose and write the email the way you always send it. Use placeholders like [Name], [date], [amount]. Click the three dots (More options) at the bottom of the draft → Templates → Save draft as template → Save as new template → give it a short name like "Invoice sent".

3. Use it

Click Compose (or Reply) → three dots → Templates → click the template name. The text drops in. Swap the [placeholders], hit send.

4. Need help writing them? Paste this into ChatGPT

I'm an assistant who sends these emails every week: [list them in a few words, e.g. invoice sent, reschedule a meeting, confirm receipt]. Write each one as a short, friendly, professional email template under 80 words. Use placeholders in square brackets like [Name] and [date]. Keep it calm and clear, no buzzwords.

5. Start with 5

Pick the emails you typed most this week. Ten minutes and you're done.

Using Outlook?

Outlook has a similar "My Templates" feature in Outlook on the web and the new Outlook; look for it in the message's … menu or under Apps. Menus can differ by version.

A note on privacy

Templates live in your own email account. If you use ChatGPT to draft them, don't paste real client names, addresses, invoice numbers or anything confidential. Use placeholders, fill in the details inside your email, and follow your company's rules on AI tools.

How many "small changes" did you do for free on your last project?

Change Order Email Template for Interior Designers: Get Client Changes Approved in Writing

By Mrs. Autopilot · 4 min · No coding

"Could we just move the island a little?" It sounds small. Then it's extra plumbing, a new drawing, a reorder and a week's delay, and because it was agreed on site, it's never invoiced. Writing a proper change email takes twenty minutes, so it often doesn't happen at all.

A change order doesn't need to be a big document. A short email with what changes, what it costs, the new date and a one-line approval is enough to keep everyone on the same page. One prompt writes it while you're still on site.

Here's the fix

1. Jot the change in one line

On site or right after the call, write a quick note: what changes, extra cost (or "cost to confirm"), time impact, who's affected. For example: "move island 40 cm left, extra plumbing, +$650, +5 days, joiner to redraw".

2. Paste this prompt

Open Claude or ChatGPT (a free account works), start a new chat and paste this prompt:

I'm an interior designer. Turn my note into a short, friendly change order email to my client. Structure: one sentence summary of the change; What changes; Extra cost (or "to be confirmed by [date]" if unknown); New timeline and what it affects; Next step. End with: "Reply APPROVED and we'll go ahead." Calm, professional, not defensive. Under 120 words. Don't invent numbers; if something is missing from my note, write [to confirm]. Add a one-line subject starting with "Change request:". My note: [paste your note]

3. Check and send

Check the cost, dates and scope against your note and quotes. Fill any [to confirm] gaps. Send it the same day, while everyone remembers the conversation.

4. Don't start until it's approved

Wait for the written "APPROVED" before you order, redraw or tell trades to go ahead. Save the email in the project folder, and copy the change to your invoice list so it actually gets billed.

Good to know

This email supports, but doesn't replace, the change process in your client contract. If your contract says changes need a signed form, use the email to agree the details and then send the form.

A note on privacy

Don't paste client home addresses, phone numbers, contract values you're not allowed to share or anything confidential into an AI chat. Use first names or initials and add the real details in your own email. Follow your studio's and your clients' rules on AI tools.

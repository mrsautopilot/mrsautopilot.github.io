Do you dread writing the same "Happy holidays, hope you..." email to every client and contractor?

Holiday Email Template for Interior Designers: Clients, Past Clients and Trades in 40 Minutes

By Mrs. Autopilot · 4 min · No coding

It's December. Your current clients should hear from you before the break, past clients are your best source of referrals, and your joiner, stone fabricator and upholsterer kept your projects on track all year. That's easily forty emails. At five minutes each it's over three hours, in the busiest week of the year.

You don't need forty different emails. You need three good ones, each with a spot for one personal line. One prompt writes them.

Here's the fix

1. Make a quick list in three groups

Open a note or spreadsheet and list: current clients (project still running), past clients (finished projects) and trades and suppliers. Next to each name, jot one thing from this year, like "the green kitchen" or "saved us on the stair rail". That's your personal line.

2. Paste this prompt

Open Claude or ChatGPT (a free account works), start a new chat and paste this prompt:

I'm an interior designer. Write 3 short holiday emails I can send before the end-of-year break. Version 1: a current client whose project is still running; mention when we pick things up in January. Version 2: a past client whose project we finished; warm, and one soft line that I'd love to help them or friends again. Version 3: a contractor or supplier I work with; thank them for their work this year. Each email: under 80 words, warm and professional, no religious references, no sales pitch. Include placeholders [Name] and [one personal line about our work this year], and one sentence about my studio's holiday dates: [e.g. closed Dec 23 - Jan 5]. Add a one-line subject for each.

3. Check the three versions

Do they sound like you? If not, reply "warmer", "shorter" or "more formal" in the same chat. Check your holiday dates.

4. Save them as Gmail templates

In Gmail: gear icon → See all settings → Advanced → Templates → Enable → Save Changes. Compose, paste version 1, click the three dots → Templates → Save draft as template → Save as new template, and name it "Holiday - current". Repeat for the other two.

5. Send them one by one

For each person: Compose → three dots → Templates → pick the version → swap in the name and the personal line → send. About a minute each. Send individually, not as one group email with everyone in CC.

A note on privacy

Don't paste client home addresses, phone numbers, contract values you're not allowed to share or anything confidential into an AI chat. Use first names or initials and add the real details in your own email. Follow your studio's and your clients' rules on AI tools.
